Selling Your Indianapolis Home This Spring? Start Your Prep Plan Now, and Pay for It at Closing

What should you be doing this winter if you want your home on the market, and looking its best, in the spring?

Field Guide · Winter-to-Spring Timeline · Indianapolis & Suburbs

The short answer: if you want to list in the spring, make your decisions this winter. Walk the home, decide what's worth fixing, line up your pros, and do the inside work while it's cold. Then the spring is for finishing touches, photos, and going live.

Most sellers who want to list in the spring start thinking about it in February. By then, the calendar is already working against them. The painter you like is booked out, the stager has a waitlist, and the "small" repair list from the walkthrough turns into a rush job the week before photos.

The other thing that stops people is money. Paint, staging, a few repairs, and new mulch out front can add up quickly, and most sellers don't love the idea of writing those checks before the house has sold. That's the gap a new program called eXp Ready was built to close, and it's why I'm encouraging sellers to start planning now, in November, instead of in March. You don't have to do any work this winter. You do want to make your decisions this winter.

Why the planning starts in November
  • You get on the calendar first. Good painters, flooring installers, and stagers don't have open schedules the week you decide to list. Booking in January for February work is a very different experience than calling in March.
  • You spend on the right things. A walkthrough now gives us time to separate the updates buyers in your price range will pay for from the ones that won't come back to you. (I wrote about that in What to Repair Before Selling a Home in Indianapolis.)
  • Inside work fits the winter. Interior paint, flooring, repairs, and decluttering can happen in the colder months, so the spring can be saved for the exterior, curb appeal, staging, and photos.
  • You launch on your timeline, not the contractor's. When the prep is done before the market picks up, you choose your list date instead of letting the work choose it for you.

What to Fund, and What to Skip

The projects that usually move the needle are the simple ones: interior paint, staging, a deep clean, curb appeal, and the repairs that would show up on an inspection. Major remodels rarely pay for themselves right before a sale.

Just because you can access up to $50,000 doesn't mean you should spend it. You repay what you use, plus the fee and interest, so every dollar should have a reason. If a project won't help your home sell faster or for more, I'll tell you to skip it.

Quick Answers

When should I start preparing my house to sell in the spring?

Start the planning in late fall or early winter. Do the inside work in January and February, and save the exterior, staging, and photos for when the weather turns.
